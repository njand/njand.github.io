Multilingual Grammatical Error Correction Feedback

LLM grammar feedback adapted to learner proficiency, shipped in 41 languages to 5,000+ learners a year after automated and educator evaluation.

Problem

Novice learners across 60+ languages needed grammar feedback pitched at their level, and every language had to meet safety and accuracy thresholds before release.

Approach

Few-shot prompting with Pydantic structured outputs via the OpenAI API, an automated multi-model evaluation harness, and human-in-the-loop review by educators.

Technical details

Built an LLM-powered grammatical error correction and pedagogical feedback engine, adapted to learner proficiency, for novice learners across 60+ languages. It pairs an automated multi-model evaluation harness and a prompt engineering pipeline with an educator-driven QA process that benchmarked outputs against safety and accuracy thresholds.

  • Structured outputs and automated few-shot curation: Prompt pipelines automatically generated and vetted few-shot examples for low-resource languages. Pydantic schemas, enforced through OpenAI Structured Outputs, kept every correction and explanation 100% parseable.
  • Cross-model evaluation harness: Compared multiple LLMs and providers with an automated multi-run harness. Multi-pass inference over educator-authored 10-sentence paired test sets (correct vs. learner-error variants) accounted for non-deterministic model behavior.
  • Educator coordination: Coordinated evaluation with dozens of qualified teachers and tutors across 60+ languages to score model feedback, analyze error patterns, and isolate language-specific failure modes.
  • Iteration to release: Iterated on model selection, prompt design, and few-shot examples for failing languages until they met release criteria. Languages shipped only after educator ratings cleared thresholds tracked in automated scoring dashboards.

Low-Resource Latin Speech Recognition

Cut word error rate from 13.2% to 8.7% on 10 hours of hand-transcribed Latin, then released the models, a post-processing dataset, code, and a live demo.

Problem

Latin in restored classical pronunciation is a low-resource language for speech: the training data is just 10 hours of hand-transcribed audio.

Approach

Fine-tuned wav2vec2-xls-r-300m on Modal, added Optuna-tuned KenLM decoding, and trained a latin-bert labeler to restore casing and punctuation.

Technical details

An end-to-end pipeline and post-processing engine for Latin speech recognition in restored classical pronunciation, fine-tuning self-supervised speech representations and sequence labelers on custom datasets.

  • Acoustic performance: 13.20% strict WER / 2.75% strict CER (5-fold CV, acoustic model only; 11.54% normalized WER) from a fine-tuned wav2vec2-xls-r-300m trained on 10 hours of hand-transcribed speech.
  • LM decoding and Optuna tuning: Optuna-tuned KenLM constrained decoding (pyctcdecode, lexicon of fully inflected Latin forms) cut mean WER from 13.20% to 8.70% (34% relative) and CER from 2.75% to 2.08% across all 5 folds.
  • Contextual BERT rescoring: Implemented a masked-LM pseudo-log-likelihood rescoring pass using latin-bert to re-rank candidate beams. Not separately benchmarked: latin-bert's pretraining corpus likely overlaps the evaluation text, so a clean measurement isn't possible.
  • Casing and punctuation restoration (ITN): Fine-tuned latin-bert as a 14-class sequence labeler (casing × trailing punctuation): casing F1 0.92, punctuation F1 0.69, macro F1 0.65 on a 95/5 holdout (98% / 94% token accuracy). Punctuation restoration involves real judgment calls, so even trained humans won't reach 1.0. Published to PyPI as latin-itn.
  • Quantization tradeoffs: ASR weights exported to ONNX INT8 for a 72% size reduction (1.26 GB to 355 MB) and ~3x faster CPU inference. For the post-processor, INT8 sped inference 2.2x but cut punctuation F1 by about 11 points, masked by stable top-line accuracy, so I recommend FP32 for quality-critical use.
  • Released ITN dataset: ~11M tokens / ~360k samples built with linguistically informed normalization: praenomen expansion with grammatical agreement, Roman numeral to cardinal conversion, and classical i/u orthography.
  • Serverless MLOps and telemetry: Automated remote multi-GPU training on Modal (NVIDIA L4 instances) with persistent caching, Weights & Biases experiment tracking, CodeCarbon emissions logging, and GitHub Actions CI.

The speech dataset is private for copyright reasons; models, the post-processing dataset, and code are public.
